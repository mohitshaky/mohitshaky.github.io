Selected casework

Client names and system details are withheld under confidentiality. What follows is the shape of the problem and the approach taken.

Telecom  ·  order management

Orders piled up every time marketing ran a campaign

The problem. Fine at normal volume. The moment a campaign launched, the order pipeline backed up and support started fixing things by hand.

What I did. Traced the pipeline to find where events were really queueing, rebuilt the consumer groups and partitioning around the correct key, moved slow downstream calls off the poll loop, and added a retry topic so one bad message could no longer block a partition.

Outcome. Campaign peaks absorbed without anyone intervening, and stuck orders became an alert instead of a customer complaint.

Banking  ·  account events

Nobody could prove the audit trail was complete

The problem. Answering one compliance question meant querying three stores and reconciling them by hand, with no guarantee the result was right.

What I did. Made consumers idempotent on a stable event ID, made the write path append-only, and split the audit projection from the operational read model so either could be rebuilt without touching the other.

Outcome. History reconstructible from the event log alone. Compliance questions answered from the projection instead of from an engineer's afternoon.

Telecom  ·  subscriptions

Changing one business rule meant releasing six services

The problem. Subscribe, upgrade, suspend and cancel logic had spread across services over several years. The actual sequence existed only in people's heads.

What I did. Modelled the lifecycle as an explicit BPMN process so the sequence became something you could read and review, kept the integration event-driven, and made every transition individually traceable.

Outcome. Rule changes became a process-definition edit rather than a coordinated multi-service release.
