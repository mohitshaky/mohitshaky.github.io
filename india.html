Selected casework

Seven years on telecom and banking production systems. Client names withheld under NDA.

Tier-1 telecom  ·  order management

Orders piled up every time marketing ran a campaign

The problem. Fine at normal volume. The moment a campaign launched, the order pipeline backed up and support started fixing things by hand.

What I did. Traced the pipeline to find where events were really queueing, rebuilt the consumer groups and partitioning around the correct key, moved slow downstream calls off the poll loop, and added a retry topic so one bad message could no longer block a partition.

Outcome. Campaign peaks absorbed without anyone intervening, and stuck orders became an alert instead of a customer complaint.

Same pattern, public code: order-lifecycle-bpmn

Banking  ·  account events

Nobody could prove the audit trail was complete

The problem. Answering one compliance question meant querying three stores and reconciling them by hand, with no guarantee the result was right.

What I did. Made consumers idempotent on a stable event ID, made the write path append-only, and split the audit projection from the operational read model so either could be rebuilt without touching the other.

Outcome. History reconstructible from the event log alone. Compliance questions answered from the projection instead of from an engineer’s afternoon.

Same pattern, public code: banking-account-service

Telecom  ·  subscriptions

Changing one business rule meant releasing six services

The problem. Subscribe, upgrade, suspend and cancel logic had spread across services over several years. The actual sequence existed only in people’s heads.

What I did. Modelled the lifecycle as an explicit BPMN process so the sequence became something you could read and review, kept the integration event-driven, and made every transition individually traceable.

Outcome. Rule changes became a process-definition edit rather than a coordinated multi-service release.

Common questions

What does a “fractional backend lead” actually do?

The work a senior backend engineer does that is not writing code: deciding the architecture before you build it, reviewing what the team ships, sitting on hiring panels so you stop guessing, keeping the codebase in a state that survives due diligence, and telling you honestly where AI helps and where it is a distraction. Ten to twelve hours a week, every week, so the context stays warm.

Why not just hire an agency, or a full-time engineer?

An agency gives you a developer and an account manager, and the person you brief is not the person who writes the code. A full-time senior backend lead in NCR is a salary plus ESOPs plus a three-month notice period, which is a lot of commitment at seed stage. This sits between the two: one senior person, direct, monthly, cancellable. When you genuinely need a full-time lead I will tell you, and I will help you hire them.

Is GST extra?

Yes. Every price on this page is exclusive of GST; 18% applies to Indian clients. If you are GST-registered it is fully claimable as input credit, so the effective cost to you is the listed price.

How do payments work?

50% advance and the balance on milestone acceptance, Net 15. UPI, NEFT or Razorpay — whichever suits your finance process. Retainers are invoiced monthly in advance.

Do you work on site?

I am in Gurgaon, so yes when it is genuinely useful — kickoff, architecture sessions, hiring panels, incident post-mortems. Anywhere in Gurugram, Delhi or Noida. The rest is remote, because a whiteboard is worth a commute and a status update is not.

We are pre-revenue and the budget is small. Is it worth talking?

Start with the ₹40,000 Health Check. You get a written account of what is wrong with your backend and what to do about it in priority order, and the fee comes off anything you book within thirty days. If even that is out of reach right now, say so on the call — I would rather scope something down than not have the conversation.

Who owns the code?

You do. Full IP assignment on final payment — code, documentation, configuration. The one carve-out is my own pre-existing reusable libraries, which are licensed to you for the delivered work rather than assigned.

Can you help us interview and hire backend engineers?

Yes, and it is one of the more valuable things in the fractional arrangement. I will write the role, build a technical screen that tests what the job actually needs, sit on panels, and give you a straight read afterwards. Seven years of production Java means I know what a strong answer sounds like.
